==========================================================================
CROSSOVER SEMANTICS
==========================================================================

For every future state defined by:

t = future date
S = future underlying price

calculate:

Spread Return(t, S)

and:

Comparator Return(t, S)


If:

Comparator Return(t, S) > Spread Return(t, S)

the point belongs to the Comparator higher-return region.


If:

Spread Return(t, S) > Comparator Return(t, S)

the point belongs to the Spread higher-return region.


If:

Spread Return(t, S) = Comparator Return(t, S)

the point belongs to the Crossover Boundary.


IMPORTANT

- The crossover is NOT one scalar crossover point.
- It is a Time x Price boundary.
- Do not assume the boundary is straight.
- Do not assume the boundary is monotonic.
- The calculated boundary may curve.
- The calculated boundary may break into multiple visible segments.
- The calculated boundary may enter or leave the visible Heatmap.
- Render the actual calculated result.
- Do not force the result to match this example drawing.
- Do not label every Heatmap cell WIN / LOSE.
- Do not create a second duplicated Heatmap.
- Existing Spread return values remain the primary Heatmap values.
- The crossover line is an overlay.

==========================================================================
UI PRINCIPLE
==========================================================================

Do not solve MVP V3 by adding many new large cards.

The goal is to improve the information hierarchy of the existing
Spread Analysis Workspace.

Historical IV should be integrated into the candidate analysis flow.

Crossover should be integrated into the existing Heatmap.

Compress:

- whitespace
- repeated labels
- decoration
- unnecessary section height

Do NOT compress away decision-critical financial information.


==========================================================================
MOBILE PRINCIPLE
==========================================================================

- Stack the same information vertically where necessary.
- Preserve the same information priority.
- Keep Normalized Skew visually above Buy / Sell IV.
- Buy / Sell IV may be placed side by side when screen width allows.
- Historical IV charts should remain compact.
- Do not create three large full-size charts if compact sparklines work.
- Do not create a second Heatmap for crossover comparison.
- Preserve existing navigation behavior.
- Preserve return-to-list state and scroll behavior.
- Do not break the high-density Mobile UX established in MVP V2.


==========================================================================
DESIGN INTENT
==========================================================================

After opening one Spread candidate, the user should be able to answer:

1. Which Spread am I looking at?

2. What is the target return?

3. How much does it cost to enter?

4. Where is the current Normalized Skew relative to its history?

5. Where is the Buy Leg IV relative to its history?

6. Where is the Sell Leg IV relative to its history?

7. How have those three IV measures moved over time?

8. What is the Spread return at different future dates and prices?

9. If the price thesis happens earlier, where does the Comparator
   have the higher return?

10. As time approaches expiry, where does the Spread begin to overtake?

The final result should still feel like one coherent
SPREAD ANALYSIS WORKSPACE.

It should not feel like several unrelated tools stacked together.
